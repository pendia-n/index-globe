# Index Globe — product and implementation plan

Updated: 20 November 2026. This is a planning document; it does not assert that the product, partners, market access, deployment, or transactions already exist.

## Vision and position

Index Globe aims to become a global platform for commercial energy and environmental-attribute transactions: discovering opportunities, specifying requirements, comparing offers, agreeing terms, coordinating delivery and settlement, and retaining evidence. Its initial users may be buyers, sellers, procurement advisers, factories, generators, utilities, and authorized intermediaries. The long-term infrastructure opportunity is to make recurring procurement and its records reliable across multiple markets. Global reach means supporting different products and jurisdictions as access and demand are established; it does not mean the platform can itself deliver electricity across borders or make every trade lawful everywhere.

The original `idiot.md` proposes global energy trading, blockchain, AI, IoT, and a range of revenues. Preserve it as the broad vision, but treat its market-size forecasts, monopoly claims, regulatory-arbitrage thesis, eight-year outcomes, and automatic claims of autonomy as unverified. The IndexGlobe chat developed a more concrete route: sell an auditable, completed procurement workflow, not just a dashboard or a list of bids. A working transaction, repeat use and unit economics are necessary evidence for infrastructure ambitions. Singapore was researched as one possible first market; it is a launch hypothesis, not a geographic restriction. Select the first jurisdiction and product based on actual buyer demand, authorized counterparties, accessible data, and legal feasibility; another market can come first if it has stronger evidence.

## Product scope: one platform, distinct markets

Each market needs its own product specification, permissions, unit, evidence, and delivery partner. Do not imply that purchasing environmental attributes physically routes power to a buyer.

| Market/product | Initial role for Index Globe | Delivery, authority and dependencies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renewable electricity certificates, including I-REC(E) or local equivalents | Request and compare quotes for issued attributes by country, source, vintage, volume, beneficiary and date; track contract and evidence. | Approved registry participant transfers or redeems; applicable registry and claims rules determine status. A certificate is distinct from electricity and carbon offsets. |
| Physical electricity and power-purchase agreements | Support sourcing, tenders, comparison and contract management for solar, wind, hydro and other generation where permitted. | Licensed suppliers, grid operators, market participants and contracts provide physical supply, network access and settlement. Electricity cannot be shipped across continents merely by recording a transaction. |
| Flexibility, battery services and demand response | Later, arrange contracts for a defined site, interval, baseline, delivery and payout. | Authorized aggregator/operator, site consent, accepted meter data and settlement route are essential. |
| Gas, renewable gas, hydrogen and heat | Later, provide procurement workflows for specified quantity, quality, delivery location and period. | Supplier, carrier/network and metering parties deliver and verify; environmental attributes require separate proof. |
| Carbon credits | Potential later marketplace with its own eligibility and evidence model. | Independent credit standards, registries, legal and claims review. Renewable generation does not automatically create an offset; do not conflate credits with certificates. |

The platform can enforce its own permissions, approval thresholds, quote deadlines, recordkeeping and evidence checks. Contract enforceability, registry action, payment and physical performance depend on law and external parties. The platform is not automatically a licensed exchange, payment provider, registry, grid operator, or central counterparty. Verify the role in each product and jurisdiction before live operation.

## Intended user journey

1. A buyer, seller or authorized adviser selects a jurisdiction and product. The public site explains the market, eligibility, fees, process and whether a figure is live, delayed, historical or illustrative.
2. A buyer creates a private request for quotation (RFQ): product, site/country, volume and units, period or vintage, required source/attributes, delivery date, beneficiary, budget/approval rules and any mandatory evidence. Explain that a procurement adviser may represent a company; use “buyer” and “seller” rather than unexplained “desk.”
3. Authorized sellers receive the same specification and submit private, versioned offers with total price, currency, fees, expiry, delivery terms, exceptions and whether each offer is indicative or firm. Do not expose competitors' confidential offers.
4. The buyer compares like-for-like quotes, records approvals and selects an offer. The parties establish identity, signing authority, terms, remedies, counterparties and exactly when acceptance becomes binding. The system keeps an audit trail and does not represent a demo click as a legal trade.
5. The parties execute the agreed contract and payment through approved existing channels. For a certificate pilot, the approved registry participant transfers or redeems as agreed. Record invoices, payment reference and registry evidence only where the parties authorize it.
6. Check quantity, vintage, beneficiary, reference and other product-specific terms against the evidence. Mark discrepancies as unresolved and let the operator correct or escalate them. Completion requires the agreed proof; provide the parties an exportable transaction record.
7. The customer returns for a later procurement cycle, reusing approved specifications, permissions and supplier records. Repeat paid transactions and a measured reduction in effort are the evidence of value.

## Commercial model, market research and marketing

**Initial offer:** help a qualified buyer conduct a bounded tender and obtain comparable offers, a documented approval and delivery evidence. The earlier chat suggested a Singapore procurement adviser serving manufacturers buying already-issued domestic solar certificates as one accessible hypothesis, approaching the buyer first and recruiting at least two eligible sellers against an actual requirement. A committed buyer and authorized counterparties elsewhere could justify selecting another starting market or asset. No buyer, partner commitment, market permission or willingness to pay is verified here.

**Monetization hypothesis:** test a fixed fee for a first tender, followed by a predictable subscription for recurring sites, approvals, comparisons and audit exports; allow invited sellers to quote without a fee initially. Clearly disclose registry, partner and other charges before acceptance. Consider success or transaction fees only after counsel reviews the role and customers' incentives. Other ideas in `idiot.md`—consulting, transaction fees, analytics, and later services—remain options to test, not guaranteed revenue. Do not monetize sensitive or aggregated customer data without a lawful basis, clear agreement and safeguards.

**Illustrative unit economics from the prior chat, not forecasts:** 20 customers at S$500/month = S$120,000 annual revenue; at S$100 direct monthly cost the contribution is S$400/customer/month, so S$120,000 annual fixed costs require 25 customers before other omitted costs. At S$250 direct cost, 40 customers are needed. Validate price, direct costs, support time, customer acquisition, churn, tax and collection costs. An illustration using 1% of €176.7 million in Italian turnover at a 1% fee yields about €17,670 before costs; the calculation does not establish accessible market or volume.

**Go-to-market:** interview procurement advisers, industrial energy buyers, suppliers, generators, registry participants and relevant market operators in candidate jurisdictions. Ask for an anonymized recent procurement, time spent, missed requirements, current alternatives, decision authority, upcoming funded demand, and willingness to pay. Explain the offer as comparable quotes plus trustworthy proof and a simpler repeat process. Use targeted direct outreach and relevant energy/industry events, publish source-linked explainers and market data, and develop distribution partnerships only where both sides have a clear role. Test messaging and channel response; do not claim SEO, social media, press, a partner, or a pilot has established demand. Show how the workflow is better than existing brokers, suppliers, marketplaces or spreadsheets.

**Metrics:** qualified funded buyer requirements, authorized sellers per tender, comparable quotes, time and direct cost per completed transaction, offer-to-completion rate, evidence mismatch rate, paid repeat tenders, customer retention and contribution margin. Track sources and definitions. Registered users and posted demo offers are not completed transactions.

**Evidence gates:** discovery interviews → named buyer and funded requirement → agreed data rights and qualified counterparties → legally reviewed live transaction → verified delivery and reconciliation → repeat paid transaction → repeatable economics. A sample or pilot alone is not proof of broad demand. Stop or change market if buyers will not commit, authorized inventory/evidence is unavailable, regulation blocks the role, or transaction economics do not work.

## Blockchain, payments, AI and IoT

- **Blockchain:** not required for the first transaction. Use conventional access-controlled, versioned records and audit history; external registries remain authoritative. If multiple customers later need independent verification after leaving the service, evaluate occasional batch-hash anchoring, including Ethereum mainnet as one candidate, with private data kept off-chain. A digest proves record integrity/existence, not registry status, payment or physical delivery. Compare simpler alternatives, cost, privacy and legal implications; no platform token is assumed.
- **Payment:** begin with conventional invoices and bank transfers between contractual parties, subject to the agreed terms. Consider optional stablecoin settlement only for a demonstrated cross-border payment problem and through appropriately authorized providers after reviewing conversion, FX, screening, redemption, cost, failure handling and local rules. A US LLC alone does not grant local permissions.
- **IoT:** useful when measured performance changes contractual payment, especially demand response, flexibility or generator measurement. Start with existing approved meters and read-only imports, consent, timestamps, revisions, missing-interval handling and reconciliation. Hardware installation and remote control require a separate partner, authority, security and economic case.
- **AI:** optional later assistance with finding anomalies or summarizing documents, with human review and measurable quality. It must not autonomously claim legal compliance, execute trades, control infrastructure or invent offers.
- **Customer mandates and competition:** customers may set their own budgets, eligible sources, approval thresholds and offer limits, with accountable overrides. Do not coordinate independent parties' prices, bids, output or collective purchasing restrictions. Seek jurisdiction-specific legal advice before designing any potentially sensitive market coordination.
